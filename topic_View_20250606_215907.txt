While I am a fan of watching documentaries, I have never listened to a true crime podcast until now. My biggest reason for not listening is because I prefer to watch rather than listen. Spotify has many true crime podcasts, and I found one that seemed to be popular. I listened to Serial Killers produced by Spotify Studios. I chose the episode "'The Dating Game Killer' Rodney Alcala" because I was somewhat familiar with the subject. Initially, I did not like that the episode sounded like an audiobook. After listening for the first 10 minutes, I was definitely more interested. The scripting was well written and the narrator was easy to understand. I could picture the episode in my head. I listened to it on the way to work and surprisingly wanted to finish the episode on the way home. I also thought the music in the background was annoying in the beginning, but then I ended up really appreciating it. The podcast episode was as effective as a story in a book or a movie on a screen. The episode made me rethink how I want to approach my podcast episodes. I want to create better scripts and gather more research about my topics. While it's time consuming, I think gathering more information and putting it in order will make my podcasts more entertaining. Audio is also something I want to pay more attention to. Serial Killers has crisp audio and it held my attention well. While I would not used music and background noises for my entire episode, I can take inspiration and make my story flow better. By planning out each episode and editing them properly, I can continue to grow my podcast into something more developed and professional. I want to be entertaining, but I cannot rely on that to make my podcasts sound good.
