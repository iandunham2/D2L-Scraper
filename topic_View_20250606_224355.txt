I don't really listen to audio narratives in podcasts so I had to use the example podcasts that were given in the prompt of this week's discussion post. I was looking through Radiolab and its episodes and I found one titled "The Wubi Effect". It talks about this Chinese input system that was used a long time ago for typing in Chinese characters into a keyboard. This story is super cool because it takes something that seems boring and that I really did not think I would have an interest in and it makes it very compelling through the emotional storytelling and how it progresses from just a story about history into questions of culture. I think that the host does a good job at implementing a little "twist" into the story, he talks about Wubi and how its a good invention that wasn't anything too crazy but it was very useful, he didn't make it seem like minutes later he was going to be discussing the downfall of Wubi. I also like how they used Chinese citizens and answers to some of their questions, it makes the story feel more real and you can hear from people who actually used it. We hear from teachers who used to use it and why they liked it so much because it made things so much easier for them. I would say there isn't much sound used in this but when it is used its done very effectively, little clicks and bell noises help the listener really visualize what they are talking about. Editing plays a big role in the episode, deciding where to put certain clips and other audio noises is very important with the flow of the episode and helps keep listeners entertained but also following along. The host is a good story teller but I would say the episode still feels scripted.
