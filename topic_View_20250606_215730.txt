One type of podcast that I don't usually listen to is true crime. I often don't listen to it because it sometimes freaks me out if I am listening to it alone. I found a true crime podcast called Casefile and listened to an episode on Ina and David Steiner. This one was interesting because they didn't die like other true crime podcasts, but they got harassed and stalked by people at eBay for something that they wrote online in their newsletter. Upon listening to it, I found that I really liked it. It didn't actually feel like a podcast but more like a movie I was watching in my head. I like how the episode incorporated music not only in the beginning but in the middle too. I liked how at the more intense part of the story, they would put dramatic music in to make the listeners more on edge and wonder what will happen next. One thing that was interesting was that the narrator was listed as anonymous in the credits. I thought that was unusual and I am not sure if the narrator was ai or if they just didn't want to be named. The narrator was British so I thought it did a good job incorporating that into the spookiness of a true crime podcast. I imagine the production process is a lot of preparation and research. When you do a true crime podcast, you have to make sure you know all the facts and exactly how it went down. The writing also has to be structured so that there is a clear beginning and then the end should be wrapped up with how the case ended. I would also say that music is just as important so that it sets the tone of the podcast. The music has to be tense and not cheery. I think the podcaster definitely researched this case a lot to make sure she didn't leave one thing out. I know that it takes a long time to not only comb through the facts but also following up on where the perpetrators are now and what they are doing.
