I like the interview in the reading with Obama, I like it because he talks about his upbrinigng and how he was always told by society what he should do or say. He says he grew up without a father so he really had to look up to others to learn morals and how to be a man, more specifically an african american man. He says that he would absorb stereotypes of the time, he didnt really have an independently formed sense of identity yet. Then he said when he was in college thats when he started thinking about what he wanted to beleive, how he wanted to be percieved, and really find that sesne of self that he had been looking for with an absent father. He talks about how he doesnt reject the white or black sides of his upbringing but rather he embraces them without letting them define who he is and what he believes just because of race.  
The episode I decided to watch is the one with comedian Tig Notaro. In this episode she talks alot about her identity and how it is being a women in the field of comedy and states that it is typically a little bit harder for women to break out in this space. She talks alot about her diagnoses with cancer and how that event and other events like it were traumatic but they were some of the biggest shapers of her identity and why she is who she is today. I think Marc does a really good job here because he asks super deep questions that we all want to know even though they are defineitly touchy subjects, but he does it in such a way that the guest never seems to feel uncomfortable and is willing to dive deep.
