The podcast example I chose was one from "Trying Not to Care" by Ashley Corbo. In this specific episode I am referring to, the host is talking about a situation where she opened up to one of her friends about a situation that deeply hurt her, only for that person to begin hanging out with the person who did her wrong. The way she tells this story is very satisfying, she is somehow able to relate it to what many audience members have gone through and do it in a way that is both entertaining and structured. Sometimes, podcast hosts can ramble too much but, I enjoy her podcast because she is structured whilst also remaining relatable and having character. She does not include many musical interludes in her episodes, only intro/outro music because for this type of self-help podcast I am not sure where she would include interludes. If she did, I think it could take away the rawness of feeling like you are getting advice or hearing a story from one of your friends. As for editing, she does not have any sort of awkward pauses in her episodes meaning, she definitely cuts out any weird timing or pauses in her original takes. The way she edits helps the entire flow of her episodes, it makes them sound seamless to listeners. As for the storytelling, she is both impromptu and structured, she does a perfect job at incorporating both into her episode. This is something that likely took some practice and time because it can be hard to incorporate both of those aspects into your storytelling. I take inspiration from the way she is able to do this and I try to do the same in my podcast episodes. I think this is the best type of structure for podcasts because it truly keeps people engaged.
