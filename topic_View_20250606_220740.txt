The question of "What if you were deaf?" has been on my mind since childhood. When I was seven years old, I had a deaf piano teacher, which to many people sounded ironic, but she was an amazingly accomplished pianist in Europe and I was honored to have her as my teacher. Miss Jane Stuckenbruck could speak and read lips, but sign language was easier for to understand, so I grew up learning sign language while she was my teacher through middle school. Though she can't hear music, she can feel the vibrations through the piano and see her fingers flit across the keys. She experiences music differently, but that doesn't mean she can't experience it at all.
In "would you rather" games as a kid when the question "Would you rather be deaf or blind?" came up, I always chose deaf. I would rather be able to see the world and I saw how my piano teacher could navigate the world very well through being deaf. That didn't mean she didn't have her limitations. If she looked away from me during a lesson, I would have to gently tap her arm for her to notice me and I had to make sure to enunciate words that I didn't know in ASL so she could understand me, but since having her as my teacher, I've always had a deep connection to the deaf community, especially since I got to meet some of her deaf friends as well. 
I think being deaf would definitely affect my relationship with sound as in I would have to think of sound as a feeling instead of through audio. When reading a book and someone would describe an explosion, I would think of it as an intense vibration instead of a blast into my ears. It's definitely a different way of thinking.
