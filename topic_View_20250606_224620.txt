https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pczS8WyVPxE  
   In this podcast Kris dives into an unsolved murder of the case of JonBenét Ramsey. She goes into the background of the 6 year old girl, talks about the case, then explains some theories presented after the murder happened. When she is talking about the case she uses a more narrative storytelling approach with the use of sound effects, music beds, and media artifacts. Some things that caught my attention was the use of media artifacts with the use of clips from a documentary that came out about the case recently. Another thing that stuck out to me was when she used editing to change her voice while reading the ransom note to set the scene.
   Within the podcast she uses music as background music to set the tone, pauses of music when there needed to be a dramatic pause, and click sound effects being used to symbolize clicks of a camera while showing pictures of the crime scene. I usually watch these podcasts rather than listen to them, so I do recognize the use of clicks to present information relating to the topic.
   Editing plays a big part in representing dramatics in the rise in volume of the background music. I do notice that the background music would change depending on the part being talked about. While Kris talked about the background of JonBenét the music was more airy and light. When Kris started talking about the actual case the music got a little more tense and mysterious. I believe the podcast was pretty scripted, but with the occasional opinion she gives while telling the story showing her own input and thoughts on the topic. I do realize that most of her podcasts are scripted, but they feel very conversational and personal. Whenever I watch or listen to her podcasts it doesn't feel boringly scripted, but more of a friend is informing you on something they want to vent to you about.
