For this discussion, I decided to go with the scientific research type of podcast and I found a podcast called "The Science of Everything" by James Fodor. I went ahead and listened to the most recent episode which was one on diet and nutrition. In this episode, he mainly talked about the roles that different nutrition areas play such as fats, proteins, and vitamins when we consume them. My reaction is that it was very entertaining. I've never listened to a podcast where I am learning a lot of new information at once but it was very enjoyable. Even sometimes when I am listening to podcasts related to something I love like tennis, I still will periodically lose interest for a couple minutes. With this podcast though, I was always locked in and listening. For a podcast like this, I feel like the production starts with the research needed before every episode. You can't be an expert in everything, and if you're going to have a podcast based in science, chances are you are constantly going to be doing a lot of research to learn the different topics so that people will actually trust you and listen to you. When it comes to the actual production process like recording and editing, I feel like it's a little bit easier to produce. He doesn't have any special guest and it seems as though he can stop and start recording a lot instead of having to do large takes while in an interview. Also, I feel like while you're in an interview, you have to think of what to say next and think of questions on a dime. You may have to actively work to change the subject and get the interviewee back on topic, which could require a lot of editing in post-production. With a podcast like this, you can move monotonously while recording and take a bit of the workload off post production. Overall, It was a very entertaining podcast and I see myself listening to more podcasts like this in the future.
