A great example of good use of music for a podcast is Girls Gotta Eat with Rayna Greenberg and Ashley Husseltine. From the very beginning, Girls Gotta Eat uses music to create a lively, informal, and enjoyable atmosphere. The lively and catchy opening music attracts listeners and conveys the humorous yet perceptive tone of the podcast. Pop and upbeat music fits the hosts' personalities and the show's themes, which frequently discuss pop culture, dating, and relationships in a lighthearted manner. The audience of the show, who are probably searching for an enjoyable and relatable experience, finds resonance in this musical selection. 
The episodes also make strategic use of music, especially as a transitional element between various scenes. These brief musical interludes facilitate a seamless transition, enabling listeners to change their attention from one subject to another without feeling jarred. The music serves as a subliminal cue that maintains listener engagement and the podcast's pacing.
It's evident from listening to Girls Gotta Eat that music plays a significant role in establishing the mood of the podcast and boosting its upbeat vibe. For my own podcast, I would consider how the music can complement the overall tone and tempo of the content, whether it is lively and enjoyable or more contemplative.
