https://radiolab.org/podcast/internet-dilemma
This podcast was on internet laws and how free expression through laws like Section 230 allow for platforms to be protected from legal repercussions based on user-generated content on their websites.
The first juncture in the narrative that grabbed my attention was at the very beginning of the story when the subject of it, Matthew, is approached by a guy outside of his house who claims he knows him from Grindr, but Matthew has never seen or talked to this guy before in his life. The music has a curious, intriguing style of tone to it that plays into the narrative and is definitely supposed to pique the listener's curiosity.
As the story evolves and Matthew keeps seeing people outside his house claiming they know him, the music takes on a darker, almost menacing, sort of detective vibe to it, as it's this whole mystery of how and why these guys seem to know who he is and what he looks like based on some Grindr profile which is something Matthew doesn't even use.
The musical interludes play into it by highlighting moments like when they go into the explainer for what section 230 is. It has a twinkly, soft song playing that gets abruptly cut off by one of the hosts saying "Okay, so let's start there," referring to section 230. It helped to emphasize the importance of it and how it was what the episode was really about.
It's also an example of how the editing played into the construction of the narrative. The listener hears this brief legal process play out because Matthew is trying to get this to stop by confronting the company in a legal setting, but it gets thrown out because of section 230. So it goes straight into explaining it, dropping the music and taking a new focus.
Overall the story doesn't seem necessarily scripted, more like they had a loose structure of how the things they wanted to talk about and in what order, and tied it all together with some tight editing.
