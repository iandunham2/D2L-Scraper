After conducting the reading for this week, I have to say that Barack Obama stood out to me the most. I think there are not many people who don't know who he is. He's pretty famous, even by presidential standards, due to his status as the first person of color to become president of the United States. I remember that I wanted to vote for him alone for that reason. I wasn't exactly old enough to do so of course when I first heard of him but it was because of his identity as a black male that I wanted to back him in whichever endeavor he chose. Although that was how I felt when I knew next to nothing about him, and before I knew more about is actual background. Personally, I really liked the parts where he talked about his humble beginnings as someone with a lack of direction in the father department and how it led to a journey of self-discovery of sorts. I know part of the angle some propaganda has in the late years is the humble beginnings. Its practically one of the oldest tricks in the book but something about learning about it in this particular context sheds a new light to the subject.
I thought that the whole interview with ariane grande was very interesting. I know her mostly as a singer and actor not so much a vegan and I found it insightful when she got into the meat of the topic (no pun intended) There were also stuff that I learned about her for the first time such as her new york city background and her status as an Italian.
I think that there is nothing inherently bad with how he conducts his interview. I think its great that he gives the people so much agency although I think the tradeoff is that it feels like uts a way to close off topics that maybe the audience want to hear
