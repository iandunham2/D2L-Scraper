 I think the best kind of interview is primarily compelling due to the very real opportunity in the interviewer to obtain information they and their listeners would not be privy to under a normal set of circumstances. Similarly, a bigshot actor may be interviewed about an upcoming movie that only he and maybe others are aware of. For my example, I chose the previously mentioned My Brother My Brother and Me as they interview Judge John Hodgman. It's compelling because like the usual formula the four answer questions only these questions were asked with the guest (or guestpert as Justin calls them) in mind.
The interview was a fairly short with Judge John Hodgman only appearing for a fraction of the entire show. Although I wouldn't say it is too short since they still had plenty of time to get a very good discussion going.
For this interview it was relatively a informal interview. Weather the questions were from the listeners that submitted them in advance or the brothers themselves it had a level of informality that the podcast is known for. I don't think this is a bad thing either as the brothers give the atmosphere a very laid back vibe with there lighthearted jokes that we can hear set the guest at ease.
I think the most important aspect and one I think was neat is towards the end. After Justin thanks the interviewer for there time he also gives them an opportunity to plug anything to the audience which helps the guest get there name out or promote a product. Something I'm sure helps in there own career
