After reading this weeks reading, I noticed that Kumail Nanjiani's identity was very apparent. He comes from Pakistan, and one of the first things he says was about Pakistan is that "The burning cars were a good hint" that things weren't going well there. He said that he essentially grew up in Pakistan until he was 18 when he moved to Iowa. I believe this makes his outlook on the world very unique because he experienced an area of America that was a lot smaller and easier to ease into than just moving directly to New York or another big American city. 
I listened to the recent episode featuring Ariana Grande and I feel like they talked a lot about identity throughout this episode. The first thing in terms of identity that they talk about is when she mentions that she is vegan. This is a big contrast to her upbringing being that she is Italian and obviously Italian food contains lots of meats and cheeses. Right away, this is insight into her upbringing. She also said that it was an animal thing so there could be something to unpack identity wise there as well. She then talks about how she grew up in Boca Raton but how her family has New York roots. These are just a few examples of how they immediately dove into identity and how i feel like a lot of interview style podcasts have to do with identity whether they know it or not. 
Lastly, I think Marc Maron's interview style is very conversational. You can tell he kind of has points he wants to hit but he mainly sticks to the conversation and what the guest in this case, Ariana Grande, is saying. She'll say one thing about her upbringing and he will ask a tailored question to what she brought up. This allows Ariana Grande to kind of "have the floor" in a sense.
