For this week’s discussion I decided to watch a rambling discussion podcast. I tend to watch podcasts that are interview styled/ very structured because I do not have the best attention span, and those podcasts keep me engaged. The podcast that I found is called “Dropouts Podcast” by Zach Justice and it includes Tara Yummy and her ex-boyfriend Jake. The podcast episode is entitled “This Week We All Cry Together.” My reaction to this podcast was disinterest. It is just not my type of podcast and the things they talked about were not things I would want to really hear/ listen to. It was just cruder than I was expecting. However, when it comes to my reaction to the style itself, I think that it was a lack of focus. Something that I realized as I watched was that rambling discussion podcast is that they are fitting for people that want something on in the background. This is because there is not really a structure to follow along to that keeps you engaged. Throughout the podcast episode they just talked the whole time about any subject that came to their minds. You can go in and out of listening and still be able to understand and pick up in the conversation they are having. When it comes to the production process of making this podcast episode, I think that it is lengthy/ professional. Though it is a laid-back podcast itself, you can tell that the production is high-quality. You can watch the episode on YouTube so we can see their set up and all their microphones, headphones, and cameras with nice quality. Also, they are filming in a nice vintage/ retro podcast studio. I just imagine that they take a lot of time making sure everything looks and sounds nice and is of top quality, making the production process longer. Also, they have multiple camera shots too which probably makes editing take longer. They have a shot of the Interviewer (Zach), the two guests sitting next to each other (Jake and Tara), each of the guests alone, and a wide shot of every person. I do not think that a lot went into preparing this podcast episode just because there is not much structure to it. They obviously had to align schedules, get their equipment perfect, and their editor in place. However, it feels more so like they showed up on the set and started filming together.
