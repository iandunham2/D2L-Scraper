A type of podcast I almost never listen to is sports commentary, mostly because I do not care for sports. But for the sake of this assignment, I gave it a shot and listened to an episode of The Bill Simmons Podcast.
I won’t lie, I was bored. Not because the hosts weren’t energetic or engaged, but because I simply had no interest in what they were talking about half the time. They jumped right into analyzing recent games and debating player stats, which reminded me of the conversations the men in my family have. Every once in a while, they cracked a joke or got heated in an argument, which made it a little more entertaining, but overall, I found myself zoning out more than I’d like to admit.
I imagine the production process for a sports podcast is pretty straightforward. The hosts probably spend their time watching games, reading articles, and keeping up with all the latest sports drama. Then, they get together, hit record, and talk about it like they would if they were at a bar with friends. Unlike a highly scripted podcast, this one felt more like an unfiltered conversation, with some stats and facts thrown in to keep things facutal. Editing probably just involves making sure the audio is clean, but for the most part, the vibe is loose and unscripted from what I can tell.
The podcasters likely prepared by jotting down key discussion points, but it seemed like most of it was just them reacting in real-time. If they had a guest, they probably had a few questions ready, but nothing too structured. It’s meant to feel casual and spontaneous, which I guess is part of the appeal. I get why people like these kinds of podcasts, especially if they’re really into sports, but for me, it's a no. I think sports stray too far away from my interests for me to find enjoyment in it.
