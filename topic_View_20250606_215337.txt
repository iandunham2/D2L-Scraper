In the reading, an example of a person whose identity is visible through the narrative is Barack Obama. He talked a lot about how he grew up without a father, how he was trying to find his place as a black man in America, and how he had to overcome all the different stereotypes thrown at him by society. He discusses how all of these factors shaped his identity and who he is today. He discusses how he had to figure out how to "affirm" the white side of his family and also "affirm" the black side of his family, he also talks about how hard it was for him to figure out who he was as a person without all the different stereotypes/boxes society was trying to place him in.
I listened to an episode of the WTF podcast featuring Cynthia Erivo. In this episode, Cynthia dives into how before she was an award-winning stage-performer she studied the psychology of singing. With this, she says she was able to have a better understanding of her craft and really connect her personal expressions into her singing. Moreover, she talks about how she was able to keep her personal identity while playing the part of "Elphaba" in Wicked. For instance, on the set of Wicked Cynthia still wore her fitness watch to track her steps which shows how even though she was playing "Elphaba" she still remained the fitness-committed Cynthia. Furthermore, Cynthia and Mark discuss both of their projects relating to Aretha Franklin and Cynthia notes how those roles helped her better understand herself and her craft. Overall, this episode demonstrates an exploration of identity because Cynthia emphasizes the importance of remaining true to yourself even in the biggest acting roles and emphasizes the importance of not getting "too lost" in your craft. As for Mark's interview style I enjoy how comfortable he makes his guests. He is humorous, conversational, engaging, and unfiltered with his guests. As a result, guests always feel super comfortable to share their personal stories, experiences, and challenges without feeling judged or pushed to do so.
