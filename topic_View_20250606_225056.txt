For this discussion, I chose "If You Want to Destroy My Sweater, Hold this Thread as I Walk Away from This American Life. It was a story about how a family came to be because a group of immigrant women decided to pick up a tall white hitchhiker from the side of the road one random day from the perspective of their daughter years later. It was a story that ran in their family for years. Four women from Peru were driving in a car and they were terrified as their grandmother pulled over to let a man in. That man ended up having dinner with them and eventually marrying a girl who was in the car who had just recently came to America from Peru one week prior. The specific things about the narrative that grabbed my attention were how organic the recorded conversations were. Moments when the daughter was talking to her mom about the story and would say things like "stop moving your feet like that" or "sorry I was being a brat". And I appreciate the way the editors kept that in because it made the story feel much more personal. The music was actually super noticeable in a good way. It reminded me that I should fade my music for longer periods of time or even keep the music running for longer periods of time while someone is talking or something is being read. The editing was awesome. I like the way they added in commentary after and in between the clips of them speaking in a conversational way. This story was definitely told in an impromptu way. It feels like a home video. Which I appreciate that it sounds this way because the whole point of the story was that it was spontaneous and impromptu the way these two people met and the happiness that it resulted in for their family.
