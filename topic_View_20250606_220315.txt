I remember being little and recording my friend and I playing and singing on a cassette tape. We had a lot of fun as kids listening to ourselves and listening back to it now it's a strange feeling to hear your younger self. We were very fortunate to have the ability to record ourselves and to make recordings even in video. I can't imagine having never heard your own voice and then suddenly being able to listen to a recording. My grandparents have several recordings of their voices and they had the most unique transatlantic accents. Back then they would just record and talk about family history or family matters, what they remembered, a lot of times they would record their older family members as a way to capture this moment or storytelling. One thing that really intrigues me about recording and media is the ability to capture a moment in time. I think it was probably pretty exciting to listen to a recording for the first time and realize what it actually was. Listening back to these recordings decades later allows myself and even my children to hear what their great-grandfather sounded like and some of what he went through. If I was deaf I feel that it would greatly affect my personal life as I love to dance. Dancing however can be done to vibrations and I knew several professional dancers who were 100% deaf but were still able to feel the vibrations and follow choreography. Missing out on the ability to hear your baby cry or to hear if you are about to be in danger in a situation (siren, someone calling out to you in an emergency) would be a huge disadvantage as a human. We use sound in so many different ways from a car horn honking to yelling for the kids that dinner is ready to avoid going into the other room. Not being able to hear would certainly change the way your everyday life is conducted.
