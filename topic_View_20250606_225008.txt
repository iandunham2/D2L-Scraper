This American Life 839: Meet Me at the Fair 
This week, I decided to listen to an episode of This American Life which usually, I don't listen to. I enjoyed this episode very much actually, as it was something new and interesting to listen to people talk about: the Iowa State Fair. There were various clips that caught my attention: gunshots to signify the cowboy shooting competition, the transitions between the shooting competition and the choir singing, and the children screaming as they go down the giant metal slide for the first time. I think these sounds help transport the listeners to the type of event they're listening about: a giant state fair with a hundred things happening at once. There's such a mixed bag of people at these events from old grannies and grandpas who want to ride the ferris wheel to a young group of college kids there to see the shooting competition and ride the crazy carnival rides to the young children getting to play cheesy carnival games for a tiny teddy bear prize. Musical interludes help move the reader from scene to scene, theres a gospel music sound playing as the choir singing fades out, zany sounding music when the kids go down the slide, and classical/time period music like Bridgerton when the narrator goes over the simplistic and interesting parts of the fair. I think music and sound effects, as well as their editing into the media is vital to how people receive the media they are hearing. If you edit in scary music or apprehensive sounds during the cowboy gun show, the viewer will get a negative connotation with that event versus something of an interesting sport like the light music they played instead here. The story to me seems structured, but not scripted like the narrator went from event to event that he and his crew experienced at the state fair and new the points he wanted to talk about but did not want to make it feel like reading from a paper. I think structure with things like this is always better than scripted, as it feels more raw and authentic to listeners and therefore makes it more like listening to a conversation or story being told amongst friends than a podcast episode.
