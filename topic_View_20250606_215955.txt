One podcast form I am not to familiar with is dramatized content, also known as audio drama. I never gravitated towards podcast because I have never really been a podcast person until now. While I do have a thing for visual story telling in creative content, film, an TV. I listened to an episode of of S- Town and I though this was a very interesting podcast. It is more of a investigative podcast where it begins as a true crime investigation but soon evolves into a deep emotional portrait of a man facing a lot of internal complexities in a small southern town.
My first reaction was very confused I didn't see the vision at all on where the podcast was going. But as I started to listen to it more and more it began to unfold in a very specific way almost like a novel. The storytelling aspect was immersive, and the way the hosted pieced together the narrative was phenomenal.
I could imagine the production being very specific because I believe it is based off a true story. So I could imagine the podcaster had to do a lot of researching and interviewing people. But also carefully decide how they wanted to frame the podcast so they are not only telling the story correctly, but also presenting fact to shape them into something that feels compelling and feels natural. I imagine the editing had to have been intense as well to add timelines, sound, and personal stories to keep the listeners engaged. Sound design played a pretty big part in the editing as well, to help bring the story to life and convey emotion. Whether it is background music, pauses, and silences to help build suspense.  
Listening to S-Town made me realize how much effort goes into high quality storytelling in podcast. Most definitely made me more open to exploring different types of podcast other than what I usually consume.
