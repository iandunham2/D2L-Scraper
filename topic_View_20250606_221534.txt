Truthfully, going about researching for the next episode of my podcast will be fairly easy. As my podcast follows serial killers and cases, typically I have to rely off of facts to begin with. I have not decided which serial killer I will be investigating's case just yet but, it gives me a good chance to look at the research and case files to figure out who would have a lot of studies done about them. Most of my episodes are from memory of what I remember about the case and what I read from the police's accounts or public case files. I have read a lot about serial killers over my life and typically have details in the back of my mind which allows me to just keep the podcast flowing. I do research before most of my podcasts to ensure I have the correct information. Honestly, a lot of information is out there for me to use and likely I will stick to the public case files as well as some psychological reports and more. I will also likely take a look into the KSU Library resource in order to find some articles as well and see if they have anything I could possibly add in terms of journalistic and peer-reviewed sources. Depending on the serial killer I choose to do, I could possibly add an audio or video source from a publicly released body cam footage of when officers arrested them or even possible interviews that were done whether it is with the news or during interrogation. The research will be easily integrated as I just need to mention the specific sources instead of saying the police released a statement or so forth.
