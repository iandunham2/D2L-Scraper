Hearing is one of the senses I could never go without, that next to sight. Being able to listen to my friends' voices when they tell me about the rude customer at work and being able to listen to the emotions and inflections as they speak is something I can't live without. Listening to music, especially classical and the way it swirls and fills my mind with dreamy thoughts is something fearful to me of losing. Over the summer I had briefly lost hearing for about three days because of overuse of q-tips. Those three days were miserable because I wasn't able to hear what my friends, family, or customers at work were saying to me, I couldn't enjoy listening to the muffled sounds of music, and I especially wasn't able to sleep because of the overwhelming anxiety of knowing if someone were to break into my house I wouldn't be able to hear it.  
Sound and hearing as a sense I think shapes how we see the world, because you're able to appreciate the "uglier" parts more and look at the "prettier" parts with a different lens. On rainy days we can listen to calming music to lighten the gloomy mood, or we can open the windows and listen to the pitter patters of the rain on the side of the house. Sounds shape our environment because they hold meaning to each of us for a different purpose or memory we associate them with. I love the sound of rain and thunderstorms because I used to sit with my grandfather at night and count seconds between thunder and lightnings to measure how far the storm was from us. These memories we grow up with or develop is rooted in all senses, but especially sounds that can take us back to the time and place we first remember hearing them. Life with sound is necessary to me, not to sound dramatic, and I cannot imagine going through life without being able to listen to other people's voices, thunderstorms, or even turning to the next page of my book.
