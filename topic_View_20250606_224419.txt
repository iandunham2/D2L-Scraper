For this week's discission I wanted to talk about a podcast I have no doubt already covered in my previous posts. This being Dungeons and Daddies. Below is the website where they operate.
https://www.dungeonsanddaddies.com/
The podcast itself is actually quite old with it being a few years old. But I wanted to discuss it since i think that it is a really good example of what an audio narrative can be. The premise is that four normal dads (played by four of the five co-hosts) from our world, earth, and are transported to the forgotten realms which is a realm in dungeons and dragons 5e. On the trip there the dads realize that all of their collective sons are missing and embark on an adventure to get them back.
There are many specific junctures in the first episode of this podcast that I really liked. The first of which was the moment that the dads and their respective sons were transported from their world to the next. Not only is the dungeon master (who is the fifth host Anthony Burch) a good narrator but his descriptions are provided with various sound designs that enhance the overall experience and help with immersion. We as the listener are not just told that the dads went through a portal into another world. The sound fx and the voice acting makes us believe it.
The musical interludes serve as a quick intermission for adds to play and are placed in the same cliff hangers one would see in a tv show.
The editing helps with the flow with the overall narrative. Since they are playing a taple top rpg, the less entertaining segments or semgemts irrelevant to the story are likely smoothed over or edited out.
As far as the story direction, it is a little bit of column a and a little bit of column b. The Dungeon Master, Anthony does have to plan out segments of the dnd campaign for the players. The entire game relies on him doing such. However since this is an rpg with outcomes decided by dice roles, it is impossible to plan everything out the way one would with a movie. Characters may underperform or overperform. There are times where the players through anthony for a loop and force him to improv. As for the players, they follow a similar outline. Maybe planning on what kind of arc they want their respective characters to take but not plan on specific events to play out the way they want since they all are at the mercy of the dice they roll.
