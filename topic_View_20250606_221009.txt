I see sound as something that goes beyond just hearing. It’s a way of feeling and connecting with the world. As someone who finds music to be one of their greatest joys, I sometimes find myself thinking about how different things would be if I couldn’t hear at all. Imagining a world without sound makes me realize how much I rely on my ears to understand and interact with everything. Without the auditorial awareness of everyday life or the subtle cues in someone’s tone, I’d be hyper-focused on visual details and body language. Conversations would be more about reading expressions and gestures. I’d miss out on the joy I get from listening to my favorite songs or any meme I love quoting. Even my inner dialogue and thought process would likely be completely different. It’s like a whole other world of communication that I wouldn’t get to experience.
On the other hand, not having these day-to-day experiences would make life more special. Without the ability to replay a favorite song or rewatch (listen) to my favorite videos, every sound would feel like a one-time, unrepeatable event. It would make me appreciate the uniqueness of every moment. Although, I’d probably miss the comfort of being able to go back and relive those moments later.
Streaming audio has completely changed how we all engage with sound too. Streaming allows me instant access to everything from new music to podcasts. It’s amazing to have everything easily accessible. Listening to music in the early 2000s was a very different experience. Back then, I relied on CDs, radio, and my iPod instead of streaming services. I remember the excitement of buying a new album (mainly because I didn't get to often). That era shaped my appreciation for music and sound in a unique way that I believe helped enhance my music appreciation today.
