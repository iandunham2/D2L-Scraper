I'm reviewing Radiolab's episode on Poison Control. 
Radiolab does us a lot of sound effects, music, and media artifacts throughout its episodes or at least one. If anything, it was too chaotic for me, but that's just my opinion. I get overstimulated easily.
The sound effects did a good job of setting the scenes for different locations such as the beeping of the scanner in a grocery store scene or the ringing of the phones in the poison control call center. Music was also used to set the scene such as the mischievous music when the journalist's toddler found the diaper ointment or the embarassed/awkward Spongebob-esque music when the journalist was unhappy with her husband's response to her toddler eating the ointment.
One part that I found especially creative was when the journalist was talking about how cleaning products became popular and because of them the need for the poison control call center arose. During this sequence, old commercials for cleaning products played in the background and the editing was done really well so this added to the picture and wasn't distracting. The parts I found distracting was when these randos who aren't the journalist or the interviewees interject to fill in facts and it's like, "Who are you?"
The story felt like a mishmash of events that were stitched together from the anecdote by the journalist about her toddler to visiting the Chicago poison control call center to visiting the grocery store with some guy to look at the ingredients in our cleaning chemicals.
There were also a lot of interviewees of poison control call center employees or of another lady who had a troubling experience with a young child ingesting a whole bottle of fluoride pills. I thought those were edited in good for the most part.
My biggest gripe with the episode is I wish there was one narrator and the instrument articulating someone tapping on the journalist was kind of stupid. Sound effects can be very hokey if they aren't used well.
